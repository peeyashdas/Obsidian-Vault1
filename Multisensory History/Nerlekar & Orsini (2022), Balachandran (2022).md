
**Aparna Balachandran, ‘Documents, Digitization and History ’, _South Asia: Journal of South Asian Studies_, 45(2), (2022). 339–349.**

In her article, Aparna Balachandran critiques the self - acclaimed image of archives as the repository of national or provincial legacy. Here, we have two cases studies discussed by Balachandran - 1. National Archives of India , 2. Tamil Nadu State Archives. She explains how the NAI through its discourse and infrastructure, posits itself as the trustee of the national discourse. Meanwhile, Balachandran, apprehends the erasure of the micro - histories which may not be coherent or may collide with the nationalist discourse. The double jeopardy comes with the digitization, as through the example of NAI digitization project, Balachandran demonstrates how convenient it becomes for these archives to put up a "chimera", a wholesome facade which create a sense of completeness for the historians. Besides, Balachandran warns of the political undercurrents whose influence though invisibilises but becomes stronger on digital platforms. She argues that, procedure of application and documentation and rejection of access, was a part of the history writing , however, the omission of these processes , and absence of digital traces of documents, would disable the historians from identifying these influences at play. 

The power of critique rests in knowing what one does not know, however, in the absence of the unknown its knowability is obliterated. 

Important questions raised in the article :
1. Does digitization lead to democratization?
2. Does digitization lead to elision of provenance?
3. How basing archiving practices on postcolonial political boundaries, distort the contextuality of colonial records?
4. How absence of technological infrastructure affect the digitization process?

**Anjali Nerlekar & Francesca Orsini, ‘Introduction: Postcolonial Archives’, South Asia: Journal of South Asian Studies, 45(2), 211–219.**

Meanwhile, Orsini, describes postcolonial archives as archives by non - state actors,  positing them as an alternative to the national and state archives. 

She draws from Derrida and Foucault , to opine that archives are doubly inflected concept, representing both the material and immaterial. In the era of digitization, this definition becomes even more important as digitization is blurring the lines between the material and the immaterial. When, looked at the point of sensory histories, the immateriality of the digital archives, far outbalance the material aspect of it. In my interpretation, this goes beyond the concerns of authors Orsini and Balachandran, as digitization not only threatens the knowledge of absence, by staging a chimera, or a facade of completeness, they also take away from the audience the sensuousness that materiality of archives present. This, can be interpreted as a romanticism of the discipline and its craft, however, I believe the ability to induce emotions within the viewer's psyche is where the power of material culture lies. However, this may be more true of archaeological sources than literary sources.
