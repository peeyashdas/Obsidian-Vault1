- General overview - discuss the origin of the idea, link it to the primary source (the report from 1938),

- **Methodology**: sources and quality of evidence, analytical technique, timetable, methods of data gathering,

### Project description

**Research objectives**

Provide a concise description of your intended research. State your research questions and
explain the scope and limitations of your thesis. How will your research add to the body of
literature detailed in the previous chapter? 

**Proposed research methods**

Explain the methodology and approach of your thesis. List the archives and collections you
intend to consult. You can also add a timetable detailing the steps of your work process and
allocating time for the different tasks.

**Bibliography**

List all literature and primary sources that you wish to consult and have discussed in your
literature review.

Your MA Research Proposal must be accepted by your supervisor, who also has the right to
request changes. The MA Research Topic then has to be formally registered with Dr Stöwesand, and thesis title which is named is binding (there is a form for this, which both supervisors need to sign). At the same time, you will need to register for an Oral Exam.

### Style Guide for Master’s Theses

Structure

Your Masterarbeit must consist of the following elements

**A cover page**

The cover page must include:
• Name of the institute, department and university
• Name and academic title of your supervisor
• Name of your Masters Program
• The title of your Masterarbeit
• Your full name and student ID
• The date of the submission
• The word count
• A signed statement of authorship as below:

‘I declare that this assessment is my own work, except where acknowledged in citation, and
has not been submitted for academic credit elsewhere, and acknowledge that the assessor

Signed:

Bibliography
The bibliography lists all secondary and primary sources actually used in the thesis. All
literature cited in the footnotes must reappear in the bibliography, in alphabetical order.
List your primary sources in a separate section. For formatting, see below.

Quotations
The quotation is marked by quotation marks ("..."). Quotations within the quote are marked
by single quotation marks ('...').
Quotations must always be copied precisely. The meaning of the original text must not be
changed and certainly not distorted. If there are errors in the original text, do not simply
correct them, but insert "[sic]". Spellings that deviate from today's spelling rules, but were
common, are to be retained. Modifications (for grammatical adjustment or emphasis) must
be indicated [by square brackets], or with [‘emphasis mine’]; abbreviations/omissions must
be indicated with the insertion of three dots in square brackets: "[...]".
Longer quotes (3 or more lines) should be indented and justified.

Plagiarism
A definition of plagiarism – citing someone else’s work without proper acknowledgement
can be found at https://www.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/Englisch/service/plagiat.html All
students must be familiar with the protocols of citation. Please note that the word-by-word
citation of a text without quotation marks is an ‘illegal interpretation of the rules of
citation’.

Correct Referencing

Precision is central to scientific work. Be consistent at all times in your referencing of
literature and sources.
You must use footnotes (instead of endnotes or in-text referencing) to cite works
referenced in the text. Page numbers should be used wherever possible; ‘pp.’ when
referring to more than one page.
Footnotes must be placed after the punctuation mark.
A full bibliographic citation is given the first time the title is mentioned. In the following
footnotes, a short title (author's name, the first part of the title, page number) is sufficient.
Footnotes should be used to cite works referenced in the prose, to provide transla<ons, or
to elaborate on a point not immediately relevant to the argument itself.

Fonts and Spacing
Your text should be written in Times New Roman, with 1.5 line spacing.
Use 12 point for the body text; 13 pt. for headings and 11 pt. in footnotes.
Footnotes should be in single line spacing.
Side margins 3 cm on the right side to allow for comments.
Text alignment: Justified (this also applies to longer citations, which should be indented;
indented quotes do not need “quotation marks”).
You must insert page numbers at the bottom of the page. The page count begins with the
first page of your text.
