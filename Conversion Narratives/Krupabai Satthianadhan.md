Negotiating with suffering

Time and again Krupabai is seen to be feeling ashamed of their frugal lifestyle, even though in the beginning she takes pride in it. The paper aims to understand the complexity of suffering and aspirations as Krupabai struggles through it.

Unlike the narrative of struggle and its intrinsic value in gaining salvation, Krupabai in Saguna, is unsure about the role of suffering. There is an emphasis on relief and critique of suffering.

Suffering also changes meaning across the book as it gains positive connotation and acceptability with Christianity, but is undesirable in Hindu context.

An important aspect of the narrative of suffering is how it is abandoned in favour of the narrative of glory from first generation converts to the second generations. While the first-generation convert is anxious to prove themselves as true Christians, they repeatedly harness suffering to prove their faith. On the other hand, the children of the convert redefine suffering as a transitional phase in achieving the Christian life with its promise of enlightenment and spiritual glory. This is true for Krupabai Satthianadhan.

Even more interesting to note is the way Krupabai deals with female suffering in an ambiguous manner, and has refrained from projecting suffering as a precondition for a true Christian life. This can be connected to her own struggles with mental and physical ill health, to which she succumbed despite her faith. This may have given space for her to reinvent her faith, as not a salvaging power from outside, but a strength that you harness from within.

Importance of personal faith and connection with god, and encounter with the warden who challenged her belief.  Importance of the monologue, in establishing faith. As this can be contrasted with her anecdote of the corrupt bible women.

This can be traced to the themes of romanticism and the romantic movement that rose as a revolt against the rationality of the enlightenment. 

Here one can see a critique of the performative faith, and contrast it to the writings of the first generation converts who performed their faith for the sake of authenticity.
